# CRITIC REVIEW 1 — Advisor L8 Instantiation Proposal (Selective-Risk Homeostat)
**Chain document 3 of 6** · Source: critic pass (Claude family, same session operating in adversarial role), 2026-08-19, reviewing chain document 2. Transcribed verbatim; headers added.

**Verdict: BLOCK as submitted.** The core variable choice survives, but the proposal contains one construct-validity defect that goes to the heart of L8, two places where the advisor applied his own stated principles selectively, and two operational defects that would generate instrument failures on fresh seeds. Findings in descending severity.

---

**BF1 — The proposed homeostat has no consequence pathway, which makes the stakes arguably decorative by the law's own philosophy.** L8's cited lineage — homeostatic RL, Damasio/Seth — is about variables whose dysregulation *costs the organism something*: the variable feeds back into drive, behavior, viability. In the proposal, nothing happens when realized risk exceeds R*. No mode change, no budget consequence, no corrective response reads the deviation. The consultation package's own Criterion 3 requires the system "can act to reduce deviation" — the proposal's regulation is purely feedforward gating (abstain per query), with no pathway that responds to *the variable itself*. A system that monitors a statistic it never acts on does not have stakes in that statistic; it has telemetry. The advisor's design may therefore instantiate precisely the decorative-stakes system L8 exists to fail — and pass it. **Remedy:** either (a) close the loop — the abstention threshold or another operating parameter adapts as a pre-registered function of windowed regulation error, making the deviation causally operative — accepting that this is new machinery subject to the built-to-pass worry, with a CRITIC-attackable minimality argument; or (b) explicitly petition the Principal for a weaker reading of "regulation" (feedforward maintenance suffices), memorialized as an interpretive ruling. The advisor silently chose neither; that choice is Rebecca's, not his.

**BF2 — The candidate-arm dose-response is closer to true-by-construction than the advisor admitted, and his Q7 answer soft-pedaled it.** He dismissed "calibration metric" as tautological, then proposed a variable whose regulation runs *through* the very confidence signal being noised. Selective-classification theory says corrupting the ranking signal degrades the risk-coverage operating point for any non-degenerate confidence distribution with mass near the threshold — which L7/L10 passing virtually guarantees. His claim that "monotone rise is not guaranteed by construction" is technically true (it requires threshold-adjacent flips) but practically thin: conditional on L7 and L10 passing, the positive arm is weak evidence, and the *entire* discriminative load sits on the specificity contrast. Apply the program's standing question — could the positive arm have produced any other result? — and the honest answer is "rarely." **Remedy:** the spec must state the evidentiary asymmetry explicitly: the candidate arm is a manipulation check; the specificity arm is the test. Failure to say this invites a future auditor to file the sixth instance of the recurring failure class.

**BF3 — Selective application of the "don't design the variable to pass the test" principle.** The advisor rejected index-staleness because its mirror-coupling "would have to be designed in" — then proposed designing in a next-window-risk prediction head to satisfy L14's predictive-target requirement, and (if BF1 remedy (a) is taken) an adaptive threshold to satisfy regulability. Identical move, opposite rulings, no acknowledgment. This may still be the right trade — the additions are small and their function is testable — but the asymmetry must be argued, not assumed. **Remedy:** a one-paragraph comparison in the instantiation: what machinery each candidate variable requires adding, and why the selective-risk additions are minimal and independently auditable where the staleness additions are not. If that paragraph is hard to write, the variable choice deserves reopening.

**BF4 — Statistical power and floor effects will plausibly generate instrument failures at the locked bars.** Per (seed, dose): ~4–5 windows of 40–50 queries, coverage perhaps 60–80% after abstention, so realized risk per window is a binomial on n≈30 — variance large relative to plausible effect sizes. Spearman ρ ≥ 0.8 over four dose points tolerates at most zero inversions; sampling noise alone can produce one. Worse, the hinge definition creates a floor: with a margin below R*, Level 0 *and Level 1* may both yield exactly zero regulation error — ties that make the Spearman fragile and the "standardized slope ≥ 0.2" ill-defined (standardized against what variance, when the low-dose distribution is a point mass at zero?). The M3 lesson applies: bars locked without a power analysis against the actual estimator produced two INSTRUMENT FAILURE runs and six retired seeds. **Remedy:** before pre-registration freeze, a candidate-blind power analysis on oracle/synthetic profiles (permitted under Ruling 9) establishing that the estimator can achieve ρ ≥ 0.8 with acceptable false-kill probability; pre-registered handling of hinge ties; and an explicit definition of the slope's standardization. If power is insufficient at 200 queries, the L8 battery size becomes a design decision for the Principal *now*, not a post-failure diagnosis.

**BF5 — Severity matching remains a free parameter dressed as a procedure, and the proposal's armor is incomplete.** "Equal standardized effect on the component's own output" requires choosing the standardization basis (which variance, which output metric), and that choice can be tuned — consciously or not — to deliver a memory dose that is nominally matched and actually toothless, guaranteeing a specificity pass. The advisor named this the weakest joint, then left it load-bearing with only a full-curve null band. **Remedy:** add a pre-registered *potency bar* on the specificity arm itself: the severity-matched memory dose must demonstrably degrade overall task performance (raw accuracy or coverage drop ≥ a pre-registered floor) at every level. Specificity then reads: the perturbation provably bit, and regulation error still didn't move. Without the potency bar, a flat memory arm is uninterpretable.

**NF1 — Ruling 9 ambiguity in the dose units.** "Multiples of the mirror's nominal logit variance derived from oracle/synthetic profiles" — whose mirror? If the candidate's measured variance, blindness is violated; if a synthetic reference profile, say so and specify its construction. The advisor flagged the calibration tension and then reproduced the ambiguity in his own remedy.

**NF2 — L8/L10 shared-machinery multiplicity flagged but unresolved.** A flag is not a disposition. Propose one: separate runs versus shared runs with FWFP accounting, before the ARCHITECT freezes the framework.

**NF3 — Motivated-selection disclosure.** The recommended variable is also the cheapest to build from existing components. Possibly correct *and* convenient; the coincidence should be named in the instantiation so the fresh-context reviewer weighs it.

**What survives.** The variable family (outcome-based selective risk, not calibration-as-variable), the logit-space noise channel, memory as the specificity component, the Level-0 baseline gate, the L14 operating-point/regulation-error distinction, and the correlated-stack disclosure are all sound and retained. But BF1 is not a refinement — it is the question of whether this design tests stakes at all, and it belongs in front of the Principal before any ARCHITECT instantiation begins.

**Disposition:** return to advisor for remediation of BF1–BF5; BF1's fork (close the loop vs. petition for the weaker reading) escalates to Rebecca as an interpretive gate item either way.
