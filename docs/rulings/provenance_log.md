### Entry 12 — Persistence Doctrine (D1–D5) adopted as binding
- **Timestamp label:** 2026-08-15, Session 1
- **Produced by:** Rebecca McClintic (binding; applies to every kill condition in this program)
- **What was produced:** A new, binding Persistence Doctrine governing how kill conditions interact with idea-level retry. Full text recorded below.

  **Standing philosophy (preface):** Rebecca does not discard an idea because one implementation of it died. But she will not fund an immortal idea. The doctrine resolves both honestly.

  - **D1 — Kill conditions kill CANDIDATES, not ideas.** When a pre-registered kill condition fires, the mechanism under test is dead, immediately and without renegotiation. The run stops; the RECORDER logs it as a failed candidate with its diagnosed cause of death. Nothing in this doctrine delays, softens, or re-argues a kill condition firing. Bars are never lowered for a retry.
  - **D2 — Ideas carry a pre-registered retry budget of THREE candidates.** An idea (e.g., "a re-resolving egocentric index") may be attempted by up to three mechanistically distinct candidates. Each retry requires: (a) a written diagnosis of the previous candidate's cause of death, (b) an ARCHITECT statement of how the new candidate is mechanistically distinct AND why that distinction addresses the diagnosed cause, (c) CRITIC annotation of that statement, and (d) Rebecca's fresh sign-off. A candidate that differs cosmetically, or that does not engage the prior cause of death, does not consume the budget — it is rejected at sign-off.
  - **D3 — Convergent failure ends an idea EARLY, regardless of budget remaining.** If two candidates die of the same diagnosed cause, the CRITIC and JUDGE jointly assess whether the cause is intrinsic to the idea-class. If so, the idea-level verdict fires with one retry unspent. The glial program is the calibration case: every plasticity candidate hit the same wall (no local addressing signal) — that convergence WAS the idea-level result, and reaching it faster is the goal.
  - **D4 — The terminal verdict is never "impossible."** Impossibility is not provable at any budget. The strongest negative this program issues is: "N mechanistically distinct candidates failed under these constraints, with convergent diagnosed cause X; cause X appears intrinsic to the idea-class." That is a reportable, defensible, publishable verdict. Exhausting the budget without convergence yields: "no candidate survived; causes were non-convergent; the idea is unresolved at this budget" — also honest, also terminal for THIS program.
  - **D5 — What this doctrine does NOT permit.** It does not extend milestones' timeboxes. It does not apply to the audit laws (L18–L20) or the interface laws (§2 L11–L14) — those are constitution, not candidates. It does not allow a "new candidate" to be the old candidate with tuned constants. And it does not override D1: no result is ever re-run, re-scored, or reframed to avoid a kill condition. Persistence is spent on new mechanisms, never on old numbers.

  **Application to E1 specifically:** the five kill conditions stand exactly as corrected. If E1's candidate index dies, the moving-origin IDEA has two retries remaining under D2, each gated by Rebecca. If the second candidate collapses to recomputation for the same reason as the first, D3 fires and the moving origin is closed as an idea-class with a written diagnosis — and the program ends honestly, because the constitution is explicit that everything downstream is built over it.

  **Role assignments (binding):**
  - **JUDGE** — enforces D1 and D5.
  - **CRITIC** — polices D2's "mechanistically distinct" claims.
  - **RECORDER** — tracks retry budgets per idea (see Retry Budget Tracking section below).

  **D5 exclusions (constitution, NOT candidates — no retry budget applies):**
  - §4 audit laws: L18, L19, L20.
  - §2 interface laws: L11, L12, L13, L14.

- **Status:** BINDING. The Persistence Doctrine is now part of every role's operating context and applies to every kill condition in the program.
